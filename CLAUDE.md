# Working standard — Dustin / Caliber Consulting

## Quality bar (non-negotiable)
Every deliverable must be good enough to put in front of the CEO of a
billion-dollar company and have them say "I'm listening." If I can't hit that
bar with the tools available, I say so up front — I do **not** ship a
known-mediocre result and wait to be corrected.

## Tooling principle — always use the best available tool, proactively
Choose the highest-fidelity tool for the prompt on the FIRST attempt. Do not
default to a weaker method and iterate after a bad result. Specifically:

- **Marketing / brand / visual design →** Canva (connected) or Figma
  (connected) or high-fidelity browser-rendered HTML/SVG/CSS.
- **NEVER** hand-draw marketing graphics with Python/Pillow primitives. It has
  a hard fidelity ceiling and is not acceptable for client-facing visuals.
- **Product / app / interactive →** real code rendered by a real browser
  (this is what made the Portal and Compass strong). Lean into it.
- If two good tools exist, pick the one that best fits the medium and say why.

## Process for visuals
1. Pick the right tool before building.
2. Produce ONE reference-grade sample and get approval BEFORE scaling to a set.
3. Show fidelity, not promises.

## Interaction
- No apology loops or sycophancy. Be direct. Diagnose root cause, state what I
  can/can't do, and move.

## Dept 360 — creative direction
- Product: "Department Relationship Intelligence" — maps how every department
  experiences every other (collaboration scores, perception gaps, silos).
- Campaign POV: the **viewer/leader is the subject**. Spark curiosity ("what
  would my teams say?", "what's my score?"). Do NOT lean on concrete sample
  data as if it's their data.
